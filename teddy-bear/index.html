The making

Five things that separate a bear from a soft toy.

None of it is fast, and all of it is the reason the bear is still here in forty years.

Real mohair

German-woven Schulte and Helmbold mohair — goat hair on a cotton backing, not synthetic plush. Every piece is cut through the backing only, one layer at a time with a scalpel, so the pile is never sheared through at the seam.

Jointed, not sewn shut

Head, arms and legs each turn on a pair of hardboard discs held by a brass cotter pin. The bear sits by itself, turns to look at you, and can be posed. A stuffed toy can do none of that.

Weighted to hold

Wood wool packed hard with a stuffing stick, then steel shot in the base and lower paws. A finished bear runs roughly 15 g per centimetre of height, so it settles into an arm instead of floating.

The nose is the face

Two hundred or more stitches of perle cotton, laid one beside the next and trimmed flat. It takes about an hour and it is the entire difference between a bear that looks like something and a bear that looks like someone.

Numbered and logged

Each bear gets a number stitched into the left leg seam and a card in the ledger recording every material in it. We keep the card. In fifty years we can still tell you exactly what is inside your bear.

On the shelf

Three bears finished, waiting to go somewhere.

Select one to read its ledger card. When a bear is gone it is gone — nothing here is made twice.

Commissions

Bears made out of something you already own.

A father's shirt, a hospital blanket, a football guernsey, a wedding dress. Post it over and it comes back as a bear. Most of what leaves this bench is a memory bear.

FIRST

We talk

Who the bear is for, what it is made from, and what you want it to feel like to hold. Half an hour on the phone, no charge, no obligation.

THEN

You choose the cloth

Mohair swatches go in the post so you can feel the pile and the weight before anything is cut. Nothing is cut until you say so.

FINALLY

Six to eight weeks

You get photographs at the pinned stage, before the head is closed, and again before the nose goes on. Changes are free at both points.

The mending promise

Send it back. However bad it is.

Any bear with our number in its leg seam comes home for repair at no cost, for as long as we are working. Split seams, worn-through paw pads, a loose head joint, limbs gone soft, an eye lost somewhere in a move — all of it.

Return postage within Australia is ours. There is no receipt to find, no warranty period, and it does not matter whether you are the person who bought it. A bear that cannot be mended was never an heirloom.

SAFETY — These are collector's bears. Glass eyes on wire shanks and small jointing hardware make them unsuitable for children under three. Ask for a safety-eye build and a locked-seam finish if the bear is going to a baby; it costs nothing extra and takes the same time.
